1. About these terms and our services

These Terms and Conditions explain how Shri Pawan Shakti Construction, based in Dabra, Madhya Pradesh, deals with website enquiries, estimates, applications and construction assignments. Our services include road, highway, civil, building, railway support and electrical works within the scope accepted for a particular project. Reading this website or sending an enquiry does not itself create a construction contract, employment relationship, partnership or guaranteed booking. A project starts only after authorised representatives agree its essential commercial and technical requirements in writing. Please read the applicable quotation, drawings and agreement before making a payment or allowing work to begin.

2. Written agreement and order of documents

Every assignment requires a written description of the site, parties, work, quantities, specifications, responsibilities, price and schedule. The signed project agreement and its expressly accepted amendments take priority over general website information where they differ, subject to mandatory law. Approved drawings, the bill of quantities and the accepted quotation should identify their dates and revisions. Conflicting instructions must be clarified before the affected work proceeds. A verbal request, social media conversation or instruction from an unauthorised person does not automatically change the contract. Both parties should retain readable copies of approvals, invoices and important correspondence.

3. Enquiries, surveys and estimates

An initial estimate is based on information available when it is prepared. Final pricing may require a site visit, measurements, drawings, soil information and confirmation of access and services. The quotation must state its validity, inclusions, exclusions and whether any survey, design or testing charge applies. Do not assume that an informal discussion fixes a final price. Concealed structures, unexpected ground conditions, inaccurate measurements or changed requirements may require a revised estimate. Any revision affecting cost or scope must be explained and accepted in writing before the additional work is undertaken.

4. Ownership, access and client responsibilities

The client must have the legal authority to commission work on the property and must disclose ownership disputes, boundary objections, easements and restrictions affecting the site. The client should arrange the access, working space, storage, water and electricity allocated to them in the agreement. Existing utilities, septic systems, cables, pipelines and neighbouring structures must be identified as far as reasonably possible. Survey boundaries and setting-out instructions should be checked before excavation. Delays or additional protection caused by undisclosed conditions will be discussed and documented rather than treated as an automatic open-ended charge.

5. Local approvals in Dabra and surrounding areas

For work in Dabra, Gwalior district and nearby areas, the parties must identify the competent municipal, planning, village or other authority for the particular site. Responsibility for building permission, sanctioned plans, land-use approval, road-cutting permission and utility connections must be allocated in writing. Work near public roads, railway property, drains or electrical infrastructure may require separate approval. Government fees and deposits are included only when the quotation expressly says so. No website statement guarantees a permit or substitutes for an authority’s decision. Work requiring permission must wait until the necessary permission is obtained.

6. Scope, materials and technical standards

The accepted specifications must identify the work to be delivered and the quality of materials and workmanship expected. Brands, grades, thicknesses, dimensions and finishes should be recorded where relevant. Samples and photographs illustrate an intended appearance but may vary with natural materials, supply batches and site conditions. A proposed material substitution must be disclosed and approved before use. Work must follow the approved design, applicable safety requirements and the standards identified in the agreement. Structural or electrical changes requiring a qualified professional must receive that professional’s review and the necessary approval.

7. Price, taxes and payment records

The quotation must distinguish the base price, applicable taxes, approved provisional amounts and separately charged services. GST and other statutory charges will be handled as applicable to the actual transaction and the company’s lawful invoicing requirements. No tax rate, registration status or exemption is promised by these general terms. Payments must follow the milestones and bank or other authorised payment instructions stated in the agreement. Obtain a receipt and verify changed payment instructions directly through the published company contact. Cash payments, where lawfully accepted, must also be recorded. Unexplained or unofficial charges should be reported promptly.

8. Advances, booking payments and cancellation

Our existing policy is that advance or booking payments are not refundable once work has started because planning, labour, materials and machinery commitments may already have been made. Before payment, the agreement must explain the advance, its application against bills and the cancellation consequences. Where cancellation occurs, the company will provide an account of completed work and committed expenditure relevant to settlement. No general website clause removes a refund or remedy required by mandatory law. Any different cancellation arrangement must be agreed in writing. Clients should read the payment and cancellation terms carefully before transferring money.

9. Measurements, billing and progress approval

Where payment depends on quantities, measurements should be recorded using the method and units agreed for the project. The client or their authorised representative should have a reasonable opportunity to review measurement sheets, progress photographs and supporting bills. Any objection should identify the disputed item and its reason promptly. Interim payment confirms the billed stage only to the extent stated in the agreement; it does not automatically approve every concealed defect or waive a statutory right. Undisputed amounts remain payable under the agreed schedule while genuine discrepancies are investigated and resolved through written records.

10. Variations and additional work

A change to drawings, quantities, finishes, levels, access arrangements or completion requirements may change the price and programme. The parties should issue a written variation describing the requested change, its cost, relevant taxes and expected time effect. The person requesting or approving a variation must have authority to do so. Additional work should not proceed solely on an assumption that it is free or already included. Where immediate action is reasonably necessary to make a dangerous condition safe, the site team should notify the client promptly and record the action and resulting costs for review.

11. Programme, delays and seasonal conditions

The project schedule depends on the agreed start conditions, timely decisions, access, payments, approved drawings and material availability. In Dabra and surrounding areas, monsoon rain, waterlogging, extreme heat and transport conditions can affect excavation, curing, surfacing and safe working hours. Delays caused by weather, permission problems, shortages or other events outside reasonable control must be reported with their expected effect. The parties should agree a practical revised programme and any justified cost adjustment. This does not excuse avoidable delay or a party’s own breach. A fixed completion guarantee applies only if expressly accepted in the project agreement.

12. Labour, subcontractors and site supervision

The company may arrange suitable workers, suppliers and subcontractors for the agreed scope while retaining the responsibilities assigned to it by the contract and law. The site supervisor should coordinate daily activities and identify who can approve instructions. Workers must receive lawful treatment, wages and appropriate safety instructions. Employment applications submitted through this website do not guarantee selection or authorise recruitment fees. The client should communicate changes through the nominated representative rather than giving conflicting instructions to individual workers. Attendance, labour deployment and specialist work should be documented where required for the project.

13. Safety and protection of neighbours

All parties must cooperate to maintain a safe site. Relevant precautions include protective equipment, safe excavation, electrical isolation, machinery control, scaffolding, barriers and safe material storage. Clients and visitors must follow site instructions and keep children and unauthorised persons away from hazardous areas. Work should protect adjoining buildings, access routes, public drains and overhead or underground services. Noise, dust and deliveries should be managed with regard to local restrictions and reasonable neighbourhood concerns. An unsafe activity may be stopped while corrective measures are arranged. Nothing here limits a legal duty to prevent injury or report an accident.

14. Water, waste and lawful sourcing

The agreement should allocate responsibility for water supply, temporary power, debris removal and disposal charges. Construction waste must not be dumped into drains, public roads, water bodies or land without lawful permission. Excavated earth and reusable material should be handled according to the agreed plan and applicable requirements. Sand, aggregate and other materials must come through lawful supply channels, with transport or royalty documentation where required. The company does not promise unauthorised extraction, utility use or disposal. Any discovered contamination or hazardous material should be isolated and assessed before work continues in the affected area.

15. Inspection, handover and completion records

Progress inspections should occur at suitable stages, particularly before important structural, waterproofing or electrical work is covered. At practical completion, the parties should record outstanding items, relevant test results and the condition of the works. Handover documents may include agreed drawings, invoices, operating instructions and manufacturer information where these form part of the scope. Minor outstanding work should be listed with a reasonable rectification plan. Taking possession does not automatically remove legitimate rights concerning defects. The final bill and any retention release must follow the agreement and the verified work, rather than an unsupported completion claim.

16. Defects, maintenance and warranties

Any workmanship warranty or defects liability period must be stated expressly in the signed agreement, including its start date, coverage, notification process and exclusions permitted by law. These website terms do not create an automatic two-year warranty. Report suspected defects promptly and provide reasonable access for inspection. Normal wear, misuse, inadequate maintenance, unauthorised alterations or a third party’s later work may require separate assessment. Manufacturer warranties apply according to their own lawful terms and do not replace any responsibility the company has undertaken. Urgent hazards should be made safe without waiting for a routine complaint response.

17. Suspension, termination and settlement

Serious non-payment, unsafe conditions, missing permissions or a material breach may justify suspension or termination in accordance with the agreement and applicable law. The party raising a concern should ordinarily give written notice describing the problem and a reasonable opportunity to remedy it where appropriate. Immediate protective action may be necessary for safety. On termination, the parties should record completed work, materials, plant, documents and outstanding liabilities. Payment or refund obligations must be assessed using the contract and mandatory law. Termination does not erase an accrued payment obligation, a lawful remedy or responsibility for an earlier breach.

18. Responsibility, loss and insurance

Each party remains responsible for its own obligations and for loss attributable to it under the agreement and applicable law. Insurance requirements, coverage, deductibles and responsibility for existing property should be identified before work starts. The company does not claim that every project is automatically covered by a particular insurance policy. Any contractual limit on liability must be expressly agreed and must remain subject to legal restrictions. Nothing on this website excludes liability or consumer remedies that cannot lawfully be excluded, or authorises fraud, deliberate misconduct, unsafe work or false statements about project performance.

19. Website, personal information and applications

Website descriptions and project images provide general information and must not be treated as a complete technical specification or proof of a particular completed assignment without verification. Contact forms may send the information and attachments you choose to provide to the company through the disclosed delivery service. Send only documents needed for your enquiry or application. Do not submit another person’s confidential information without authority. The existing Privacy Policy and operating rules remain available separately. Third-party websites and delivery services have their own terms. A failed upload or unanswered enquiry is not a confirmed project order.

Raise concerns through 8878128338 or shripawanshakticonstruction@gmail.com, identifying the site, agreement, relevant dates and the remedy requested. The parties should first attempt a documented discussion through authorised representatives and may agree mediation where suitable. Any court jurisdiction or arbitration arrangement must be validly agreed; these website terms do not invent an exclusive local forum or take away a consumer’s right to approach a competent authority. Future revisions apply prospectively and do not retrospectively change an existing signed project contract without agreement.

21. Website materials and responsible use

The company name, branding and website content may be used to identify or discuss our services, but must not be copied or presented as another business’s work without permission. Rights in third-party photographs and illustrations remain with their respective owners. Do not interfere with the website, upload harmful files, impersonate another applicant or submit misleading project details. Any permission to use project photographs should be agreed separately and respect the client’s privacy and third-party rights. If part of these terms is found invalid, the remaining lawful provisions continue to apply. A failure to insist on a right immediately does not automatically mean that the right has been permanently waived. Questions about permitted use can be sent to the published company email.
